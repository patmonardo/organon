# Production Artifact Rational Contract

## Purpose

This note captures the architectural posture that has emerged around the enterprise track: the system does not merely produce data or task outputs. It produces structured artifacts whose validity is derived from their linkage to a rational production chain.

## Core Position

The intelligence of the system is not primarily located in the graph container itself. It is located in the layer that defines:

- feature grammars as the essential relations that make a domain legible,
- rational language as the compositional medium that turns those relations into meaningful form,
- and plans as the procedural extension of that form into executable logic.

The graph, the corpus, and the runtime are important, but they are carriers and substrates rather than the primary source of intelligence.

## Layering

### 1. Model

The model is the durable conceptual frame that gives the production its intelligible structure.

It answers:

- what kind of thing is being represented,
- what counts as a valid relation,
- and what kind of explanation the system is trying to sustain.

### 2. Feature

The feature layer names the meaningful distinctions and relations that matter for the current production.

This is the layer of:

- feature grammars,
- essential relations,
- semantic distinctions,
- and the first formation of a rational vocabulary.

### 3. Plan

The plan is the procedural or logical extension of the model-feature structure.

It turns a recognized relation into:

- a workflow,
- a decision route,
- a stored logic procedure,
- or an executable expression.

### 4. Artifact

An artifact is the concrete product of a production cycle. It is not merely a record. It is a stabilized object carrying evidence of its formation.

Every artifact should be traceable to:

- a model identity,
- a feature identity,
- a plan identity,
- and, where appropriate, a rational principle or rule that explains why the artifact counts as meaningful.

## Corpora and Evidence Substrates

Corpora are not the primary intelligence. They are the evidence-bearing substrates that the system carries along.

A useful posture is:

- multiple corpora may exist as distinct evidence environments,
- each corpus may be represented through one or more dataframe-like bodies,
- and the graph or graph-form structure binds them into a larger relational field.

In other words, corpora are the material remembered context of a production, while the intelligence resides in the grammar and rational language that organize that context.

## Production Contract

Every production should preserve a minimal rational contract:

- model_id: the conceptual frame of the production
- feature_id: the feature grammar or essential relation involved
- plan_id: the procedural or logical expression of the production
- artifact_id: the produced object or output
- principle_id: the rational principle that makes the artifact intelligible

This contract allows the system to treat artifacts as more than dumps of data. They become linked nodes in a self-explicating production network.

## Operational Consequence

This posture supports a durable agent workflow:

1. ingest or construct an evidence substrate,
2. identify the relevant feature grammar,
3. lower that grammar into a rational language form,
4. express the result as a plan or stored logic procedure,
5. materialize an artifact linked back to model, feature, plan, and principle.

This is the basis for rational self-training, consultant-grade workflow design, and long-lived agent participation in structured production.
